# ClearTrial — Deep Research 2: The Deterministic-Eligibility Design & Agentic Architecture

Research compiled Aug 13, 2026 for Biopharma Hack Day. Companion to RESEARCH.md.
Two parts: (A) evidence for the load-bearing design choice — the LLM never decides
eligibility; (B) how to structure small role-based agents that report to a primary
data layer where the decision is actually made. All sources verified this session.

---

## PART A — "The language model never decides eligibility": validation

The design: AI does exactly two jobs (extract facts from messy text; write explanatory
prose). Every pass/fail/unknown verdict is produced by deterministic TypeScript
against published protocol text. Replayable, testable, auditable.

### A.1 The evidence that verdicts must NOT come from the model

**"Right Prediction, Wrong Reasoning: Uncovering LLM Misalignment in RA Disease
Diagnosis" (arXiv 2504.06581, 2025).** The single most load-bearing citation for this
design. On real rheumatoid-arthritis patient data, the best LLM predicted the correct
diagnosis ~95% of the time — but when medical experts reviewed the model's reasoning,
**~68% of the reasoning was incorrect**. Correct answer, wrong justification. This is
the exact failure mode ClearTrial's architecture eliminates: even when the model gets
the right answer, you cannot trust the "why" it produces. ClearTrial's
`EvaluatedCriterion.detail` is generated by code, not the model, precisely for this
reason.

**"A systematic review of trial-matching pipelines using large language models"
(arXiv 2509.19327, 2025).** 31 included studies (2020-2025). The review's named
deployment challenges: **hallucination risk, data leakage, and bias**, plus lack of
standardized metrics. Notably, it found fine-tuned smaller models rarely beat
GPT-4-class zero-shot prompting — i.e., the frontier model itself is the reliability
bottleneck, and you cannot fine-tune your way to deterministic guarantees.

**"Neurosymbolic Clinical Trial Matching via LLM-Driven Abduction and Logical
Verification" (αNeSy-CTM, arXiv 2606.20895, June 2026).** Direct academic validation
of the exact split ClearTrial uses. Quote: LLMs "still struggle with the
**deterministic verification required for complex eligibility criteria**", while
purely symbolic methods "break down when faced with incomplete patient records and
noisy clinical evidence". Their hybrid — LLM abduction + logical verification —
**outperformed standalone LLM baselines by up to 30% relative**. The paper's conclusion
is ClearTrial's thesis: neurosymbolic methods are "a path toward the next generation
of auditable, LLM-driven clinical applications."

**TrialGPT (NCI, arXiv 2307.15051).** Best-in-class LLM criterion matching still sits
at **87.3% accuracy** — a 12.7% error rate per criterion. ClearTrial's architecture
converts that same uncertainty into a safe direction: the deterministic engine treats
unknown as unknown, and any extraction doubt surfaces as "needs clinician review",
never as a false match.

**Memorial Sloan Kettering — MSK-MATCH (arXiv 2511.05696, 2025).** The strongest
clinical precedent. MSK's "Multi-Agent Trial Coordination Hub" — LLM + curated trial
knowledge base + RAG, with explanations grounded in source text — auto-resolved
**61.9% of cases and triaged 38.1% to human review**, achieving 98.6% accuracy /
98.4% sensitivity / 98.7% specificity at the patient level, and cut screening time
from **20 minutes to 43 seconds at $0.96 per patient-trial pair**. The key design
point: even the best AI-assisted pipeline in a top cancer center deliberately routes
~40% of cases to humans. The human-triage posture is not a weakness; it is the
clinically validated default.

### A.2 The honest counterpoint (know it before a judge asks)

**"End-To-End Clinical Trial Matching with Large Language Models" (arXiv 2407.13463,
2024).** GPT-4o end-to-end (no rules): 93.3% candidate-trial recall, 88.0% criterion
accuracy (→92.7% after baseline refinement), on 51 synthetic EHRs. It claims parity
with or better than physicians. This is the strongest pure-LLM counterargument. Its
limits, which the paper itself accepts:
- Synthetic EHRs only — real-world noise and incompleteness are exactly where αNeSy-CTM
  showed pure LLMs degrade.
- No replayable decision path: the "verdict" is a sampled token; you cannot diff
  protocol versions against it, unit-test it, or sign it off.
- The 7.3-12.7% residual error is invisible to the patient — there is no
  "needs_review" bucket in an end-to-end system.
ClearTrial's honest retort: "They report 92.7% accuracy. We report 23% coverage with
100% auditability and a safe direction for everything else. You can sign off our
rules; you cannot sign off a transcript."

### A.3 Why extraction-only is the right split (the design's full rationale)

1. **Auditability by construction.** Every verdict = (protocol sentence, patient
   value, rule) — diffable, versionable, unit-testable. The project's own negation bug
   ("no brain mets" read as positive) and the "sm-all" boundary bug are the canonical
   evidence: both were found, fixed, and locked with regression tests in code. You
   cannot regression-test a prompt into submission.
2. **Safe failure direction.** Null = unknown, never passes. A bad extraction produces
   "your doctor needs to confirm this", not a false match. LLM error converts to
   review, deterministically.
3. **Regulatory alignment (see RESEARCH.md §6 for the CDS guidance).** Criterion 4 of
   the Jan 29, 2026 CDS guidance (HCP independently reviews the basis) is satisfied by
   construction: the basis is a readable, quoted decision path. The FDA's
   ML-device "Guiding Principles" series (GMLP; Predetermined Change Control Plans;
   Transparency — joint FDA/Health Canada/MHRA) all reward exactly this property:
   transparent, change-controlled, evidence-grounded logic. FDA's draft guidance
   "Considerations for the Use of AI to Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug
   and Biological Products" (Jan 2025, still draft, docket FDA-2024-D-4689) shows the
   agency is actively scrutinizing AI claims in the drug-development context — the
   deterministic engine is the difference between a defensible claim and a vulnerable
   one.
4. **Cost and latency.** Rules are free and instant; the LLM is paid only for
   extraction and prose. MSK-MATCH's $0.96/patient-trial pair is the benchmark to beat.
5. **The residual risk is contained and visible.** Extraction is still an LLM
   judgment. Mitigations already in the codebase and worth extending: strict JSON
   schema + code re-coercion, closed vocabularies (prior therapy classes), explicit
   no-inference instructions (no ECOG guessing from lifestyle), patient-facing
   verification of the extracted profile BEFORE matching, and — the literature-backed
   upgrade — multi-model consensus for ambiguous extractions (see the AMIA 2026 NSCLC
   paper in RESEARCH.md §3) and an adversarial negation-review pass (Part B below).

## PART B — Agentic architecture: small agents → primary data layer → decision

The proposed pattern — *small agents do jobs and report back to a primary data layer,
which then makes the decision* — is a recognized architecture with a classic pedigree:
the **blackboard system**, revived in the LLM era.

### B.1 The pattern, named and validated

- **Classic blackboard architecture** (Hearsay-II heritage): specialist "knowledge
  sources" read and write to a shared blackboard; a controller decides when each
  contributes; the solution emerges from the shared state, not from any single agent.
- **LLM-era revival, verified this session:** "LLM-Based Multi-Agent Blackboard System
  for Information Discovery in Data Science" (arXiv 2510.01285) and "Terrarium:
  Revisiting the Blackboard for Multi-Agent Safety, Privacy, and Security Studies"
  (arXiv 2510.14312). Terrarium's point matters for ClearTrial: the shared blackboard
  is where data crosses agent boundaries, so it is the privacy and security surface
  you must design deliberately.
- **The data-layer-decides principle is also independently validated:** "AgentForge:
  Execution-Grounded Multi-Agent LLM Framework" (arXiv 2604.13120) makes
  execution-grounded verification first-class — LLMs generate, the environment
  verifies. αNeSy-CTM (Part A) is the clinical version: LLM hypothesizes, logic
  verifies. "Multi-Agent Code-Orchestrated Generation" (arXiv 2510.03902) shows
  deterministic code orchestrating generative agents is the reliable pattern. Your
  instinct is the consensus direction of the field.

**The invariant that makes it safe for ClearTrial:** agents are stateless workers
that write *typed artifacts* (facts, evidence, prose drafts) into the data layer.
The decision engine (match.ts) is code that reads the layer and is the ONLY component
that writes verdicts. An agent never decides eligibility — not even the orchestrator.
This extends the load-bearing design choice to the agentic architecture unchanged.

### B.2 Role decomposition for ClearTrial (concrete)

| # | Agent role | Input → Output (typed artifact) | LLM or code? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intake/Extractor** | plain text → `PatientProfile` (zod schema, closed vocab, no inference) | LLM (structured) | Exists (`extract.ts`) |
| 2 | **Criterion Normalizer** | protocol bullet → `RuleKind` + parameters | LLM classify, **code gate** | Partial (regex today; LLM raises coverage) |
| 3 | **Evidence Grounder (RAG)** | each evaluated criterion → verbatim protocol sentence + source | Code + retrieval | Exists (quotes in `match.ts`) |
| 4 | **Negation/Guardrail Reviewer** | `PatientProfile` → adversarial re-check (negation, abbreviations, boundaries) | LLM 2nd-pass / multi-model consensus | New — the two regression bugs are its test suite |
| 5 | **Explainer** | verdicts + quotes from layer → trial-card prose | LLM (post-verdict only) | Exists |
| 6 | **Signal Analyst** | exclusion signals from layer → Protocol Optimization Alert | LLM agent (Strands SDK) | Exists (`protocol-agent.ts`) |
| 7 | **Privacy Scrubber** | any outbound artifact → PII/identifier scan, concept-only telemetry | Rules + LLM check | New |
| 8 | **Trial Radar** (announcement monitor) | press releases, SEC filings, wires → `TrialAnnouncement[]` (drug, phase, indication, mechanism, date, source) | LLM extract + **code dedupe vs CT.gov** | New |
| — | **Decision Engine** | reads layer → `MatchResult` verdicts & scores | **Code only** | Exists (`match.ts`) |
| — | **Orchestrator/Controller** | manages pipeline, timeouts, fallbacks, parallelism | Code (thin router) | Exists (API route) |

Why these roles, per the literature:
- **#2 (Criterion Normalizer) is the highest-value new agent.** Today ~77% of
  criteria are `unparsed` because regex can't classify them. An LLM classifier with a
  deterministic gate (classified → evaluated by code; refused classification →
  `unknown`) is the bounded path to raise coverage past 23%, and it matches the
  systematic review's finding that GPT-4-class zero-shot classification is state of
  the art. Every new RuleKind stays a pure function + unit test — the model only
  suggests the mapping, code validates it against the protocol text.
- **#4 (Guardrail Reviewer) is the safety multiplier.** The two shipped regression
  bugs (negation polarity, abbreviation boundaries) are exactly the class of errors a
  second-pass adversarial reviewer catches. Multi-model consensus (AMIA 2026 NSCLC
  paper) or a cheap second model with a different prompt catches extraction drift
  without a big cost add. Disagreement → field becomes null → "needs review". Tie
  breaks are decided by code policy, never by a third LLM.
- **#6 (Signal Analyst) already proves the pattern in the repo** — the Strands agent
  (verified: `@strands-agents/sdk` 1.13.0 on npm, Apache-2.0) writes a structured
  brief from the layer's signals. It reads; it does not decide.

### B.8 Trial Radar — announced-before-registry intelligence (role #8)

**The regulatory facts that make this valuable:**
- IND applications are confidential — 21 CFR 312.130 keeps everything submitted to
  FDA under an IND confidential; FDA does not publish IND numbers (the user's
  understanding is correct). You cannot monitor INDs; you CAN monitor the public
  announcements companies make about the trials those INDs cover.
- ClinicalTrials.gov registration is required within 21 days of first participant
  enrollment (FDAAA §801) and results within 12 months of completion. So a press
  release announcing "phase 1 initiated" typically leads the registry by weeks to
  months. **Press releases and SEC filings are the earliest public signal a trial
  exists.**
- Companies announce trials via: press-release wires, investor materials (8-K/10-K/
  10-Q, earnings calls), and IR/pipeline pages. For BMS: news.bms.com (Cloudflare-
  walled), SEC EDGAR filings, quarterly business-review decks.

**The opportunity:** a leading-indicator "pipeline radar" that (a) ingests company
announcements, (b) extracts structured trial facts (drug, phase, indication,
mechanism/target, patient-population hints, announcement date, source), (c)
dedupes/merges against the CT.gov dataset (normalize drug names; match by
indication + phase), and (d) assigns a status: `pre-registration` (announced, not on
CT.gov), `registered` (on CT.gov), or `duplicate`. The researcher dashboard gains a
recruitment-planning head start and competitive intelligence ("who else is targeting
KRAS G12C / BCMA×CD3"), which is exactly the demand-side data the BMS AI-factory
storyline wants.

**Sources — verified this session (2026-08-13):**
- SEC EDGAR full-text search — `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=...`
  (free, keyless, official). Requires a declared User-Agent with contact info
  ("Undeclared Automated Tool" block otherwise; a UA like
  `ClearTrialDemo contact@example.com` passes). Verified: returns 8-K hits for
  "investigational new drug" (e.g., Heron Therapeutics 2019 8-K). Filter by
  `forms=8-K,10-K,10-Q` and `dateRange`.
- GDELT doc API — `https://api.gdeltproject.org/api/v2/doc/doc?query=...&mode=artlist&format=json`
  (free, real-time global news incl. press-wire pickups). Throttle to ≥5s between
  requests (verified: API responds, throttle message is the standard behavior).
- Press wires — PR.com search (`https://www.pr.com/press_list.php`), EINPressWire
  (`https://world.einnews.com/search`), BusinessWire / PR Newswire (bot-walled;
  reach them via GDELT or RSS rather than direct scraping). Include company IR/RSS
  where available.
- ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 remains the official registry source for dedupe.

**Architecture (same invariant as everything else):** the LLM extracts announcement
facts into typed `TrialAnnouncement` artifacts; CODE matches them against the
registry and assigns status; no agent decides "this is a new trial" alone. Runs as a
build-time/cron pipeline (like `scripts/fetch-trials.mjs`, continuous), NOT in the
patient request path. Demo: a seeded "Pipeline Radar" panel on the researcher
dashboard listing announced-but-not-registered trials with the source press
release/SEC filing cited.

### B.3 The primary data layer (the "report back" contract)

- **Typed artifacts only.** Everything an agent writes is a zod-validated record:
  `PatientProfile`, `EvaluatedCriterion[]`, `MatchResult[]`, `UnmatchedSignal[]`,
  `ProtocolBrief`. Free text never crosses agent boundaries as a transport.
- **Immutable decision log.** Every verdict appends a replayable record (trial NCT ID,
  criterion source, patient value, rule, outcome). The existing Convoke export is the
  seed of this; make it the runtime spine. This is the "auditable by construction"
  claim made observable.
- **Consensus/aggregation lives in the layer.** When N agents disagree on a fact, the
  layer records the disagreement and the engine maps it to `unknown`, never to a
  verdict. (Agent Forest, arXiv 2402.05120: sampling-and-voting scales LLM
  performance — the layer is the voting mechanism.)
- **Privacy boundary — cite this in the pitch:** "AgentLeak: A Benchmark for
  Internal-Channel Privacy Leakage in Multi-Agent LLM Systems" (arXiv 2602.11510)
  shows sensitive data leaks through inter-agent messages, shared memory, and tool
  arguments in ways output-only audits miss. Design rule: **no raw patient text ever
  enters the shared layer** — the extractor writes a scrubbed profile; the scrubber
  is upstream of every other agent; concept-level telemetry is the only thing
  retained (matches the existing no-store promise and makes the "nothing stored" claim
  structurally true, not just policy).

### B.4 Orchestration patterns to borrow

- **Pipeline with parallel fan-out.** Per-trial evaluation is embarrassingly parallel:
  after extraction, evaluate N trials concurrently (each trial's criteria are
  independent), then aggregate. This is where the latency win is — TrialGPT's
  retrieval-first stage (recall >90% with <6% of the collection) can shrink the 60
  trials to the ~10 relevant before paying for evaluation.
- **Routing by difficulty.** Cheap/fast model for high-confidence extractions, strong
  model for ambiguous ones (αNeSy-CTM showed CoT and neurosymbolic routing are
  complementary; RCR-Router, arXiv 2508.04903, is the structured-memory version).
- **Deliberation for the dashboard only.** DOVA-style adversarial verification
  (arXiv 2603.13327) belongs on the analyst side (draft brief → self-critique pass),
  not on the patient-side verdict path, where speed and determinism win.
- **Human-in-the-loop, always.** Patient reviews her own extracted profile; the email
  draft is hers to send; the dashboard flags "needs review" criteria for the clinical
  team. MSK-MATCH's 38.1% human-triage rate and the JMIR 2026 sociotechnical framework
  (LLM recruitment tools must be embedded with human oversight and governance) both
  say the same thing: the human gate is the product, not a limitation.

### B.5 Frameworks (all live-verified this session)

| Framework | What it is | Fit for ClearTrial |
|---|---|---|
| **OpenAI Agents SDK** | Handoffs, guardrails, tracing (openai.github.io/openai-agents-python) | Optional; good if roles become true sub-agents |
| **LangGraph** | Graph/state orchestration, durable state | Overkill for a serverless demo; state model is close to B.3 though |
| **CrewAI** | Role-based "crews" | Closest conceptual match to roles; adds dependency weight |
| **AWS Bedrock AgentCore** | Managed agent runtime on AWS | The AWS/sponsor story if you demo on Bedrock; not needed for the app itself |
| **Strands Agents SDK** | TypeScript agent SDK (npm 1.13.0, Apache-2.0) — **already integrated** | Keep for the Signal Analyst |
| **DSPy** | Declarative LM programming / prompt optimization | Great for hardening the Extractor (evals + automatic prompt tuning) |
| NVIDIA NeMo Agent Toolkit (ex-AgentIQ) | Agent framework on NVIDIA stack | the sponsor AI-factory storyline only; no code impact |

**Recommendation:** do not adopt a heavyweight framework. The current codebase
(Next.js serverless route + zod 4.4.3 + Strands) already implements the pattern;
formalize it as typed worker functions with zod contracts and the decision log, add
the Guardrail Reviewer and Criterion Normalizer as the first two new roles, keep
Strands for the analyst brief. The architecture is the deliverable; the framework is
a detail.

### B.6 Evaluation & safety of the agent pipeline

- **Per-stage evals, not just end-to-end.** Extractor: accuracy on a gold set (the
  demo paragraph + variations), plus the negation/abbreviation regression suite.
  Normalizer: classification accuracy vs human-labeled criteria from the 60 trials.
  Explainer: fidelity check — regenerated prose must not contradict any verdict in the
  decision log (a deterministic lint, not a vibe check).
- **Pipeline invariants as tests:** every agent artifact zod-round-trips; verdicts
  originate only from the engine (assert in tests); no raw text in the shared layer
  (assert in tests); any agent timeout/failure falls back to local deterministic mode
  (already proven: 8ms offline path).
- **Cost budget:** benchmark against MSK-MATCH's $0.96/patient-trial pair; routing +
  retrieval-first should land well under it.
- **Failure taxonomy** (each maps to a safe outcome): extraction conflict →
  `needs_review`; normalizer refusal → `unknown`; explainer contradiction → regenerate
  or omit; agent outage → offline fallback.

### B.7 Phased implementation plan

- **Phase 0 (hours):** zod contracts already exist — add the immutable decision log +
  the no-raw-text invariant tests. Zero new dependencies.
- **Phase 1 (demo-day stretch):** Guardrail Reviewer (second-pass negation/boundary
  check on extraction; cheap model; disagreement → null → review). Parallel per-trial
  evaluation in the match route.
- **Phase 2 (post-hack):** Criterion Normalizer agent for lab-value rules
  (ALT/AST/bilirubin, histology, EGFR/ALK/KRAS) with the deterministic gate; DSPy
  evals for the extractor; persist the signal store.
- **Phase 3:** Privacy Scrubber formalization; Strands analyst brief hardening;
  optional Bedrock AgentCore migration for the AWS story.
- **Phase 4 (Trial Radar):** EDGAR + GDELT ingestion script (throttled, cron),
  announcement → typed artifacts → code dedupe vs TRIALS → `pre-registration`
  status; seeded pipeline-radar panel on the dashboard; press-wire search endpoints
  per source (PR.com/EIN verified; BusinessWire via GDELT/RSS).

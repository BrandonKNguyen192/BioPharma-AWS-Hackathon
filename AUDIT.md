## Verified facts (safe to show judges)

1. **The displayed ledger uses real sources.** The 4 product-facing records link
   to official sponsor/partner releases and ClinicalTrials.gov. Each has a
   manually checked source quote; none is model-generated.
2. **Reviewed registry joins are explicit.** An `exact` link requires the same
   asset plus indication/phase/sponsor context. Automatic asset-name matches in
   `pipeline-radar.json` are only `probable` discovery candidates.
3. **First-posted dates are real.** From CT.gov API v2 (2026-08-13). The
   "announced before registry" comparison is `eventDate.sortDate <
   registryMatch.firstPosted` — a date comparison on two real timestamps.
4. **The reviewed KTX-1001 release does not publish an IND number.** The current
   automatic snapshot has 7 IND-language candidates, but they are not judge-safe until
   their excerpts are reviewed. IND submissions are generally confidential under
   21 CFR 312.130; do not claim that no company ever discloses an IND number.
5. **Sponsors are the drug owners.** Attribution is by drug → sponsor map
   (nivolumab/Opdivo/pumitamig/iberdomide → BMS, etc.), not by the filer of the
   document. A C4 Therapeutics 8-K mentioning palbociclib is labeled a Pfizer
   signal with the filer noted.

## Discovery-grade / heuristics (do NOT claim precision to judges)

1. **All automatic records are discovery candidates.** Their milestone labels
   are keyword heuristics. `eventType` (regulatory_submitted,
   topline_results, …) comes from regex classification of the clipped text. The
   quote is verbatim; the LABEL is a heuristic. Say "the filing mentions IND
   clearance" (quote it), not "FDA confirmed IND clearance".
2. **GDELT items are press coverage, not primary announcements.** They carry
   `sourceType: press_release` for display, but several are third-party news
   articles about the drug. A quote from them is real; "this is the company's own
   press release" is not always true. Prefer EDGAR-derived signals in the
   reviewed set for the judges.
3. **Evidence excerpts can clip off-target.** The clip prefers an IND statement,
   then the drug name, then any trial/phase mention in the primary document —
   good but heuristic. Always read the quoted sentence before presenting it.
4. **Drug attribution depends on a fixed lexicon.** If a filing discusses a drug
   by code name only (e.g. BMS-986XXX not in the lexicon), `drug` may be null and
   the signal lands in "no registry match" even if a match exists.
5. **`reviewNote` is template text**, not a per-signal human review. The
   "Regulatory/registry review" wording describes the deterministic build-time
   join, not a clinical reviewer's sign-off. If judges ask, that is the honest
   reading.

## Simulated / demo-grade (never present as real-world data)

1. **Eligibility friction is the SIMULATED pilot cohort** (signal-store seeds).
   The "catalyst approaching while friction high" story combines REAL Convoke
   catalyst data with SIMULATED friction. In the demo say "seeded pilot cohort",
   exactly as the dashboard already does.
2. **Convoke stage/catalyst data** is committed `programs.json` (build-time
   lookup), shown at the source's stated granularity ("Q3 2026"), not a live
   query. The UI already labels this.
3. The BMS "single-sponsor portfolio" label in `trials.json` is a fetch-time
   choice; registry meta shows some trials are led by other organizations
   (e.g. academic centers). Do not claim every monitored trial is BMS-sponsored —
   say "the portfolio contains BMS assets and comparators".
